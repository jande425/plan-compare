Settings & price

The battery is simulated half-hour by half-hour for every plan, trying self-use, solar export and cheap-rate grid charging and keeping the cheapest, then again at its year-10 capacity (2% capacity loss a year) for the 10-year figures. Same-day foresight, so a real battery will do a little worse. Solar above your export limit isn't visible in meter data, so charging may be understated for homes that already hit that limit.

How this works

  • Plans: every current residential offer published under the Consumer Data Right for the area (loading…). Prices include GST; guaranteed discounts are applied, pay-on-time and other conditional discounts are not.
  • Year 1: your half-hours priced with each plan's own rates, time windows, daily caps, feed-in tiers, controlled load and demand charges. Less than a year of data is scaled up, which can favour or penalise seasonal plans.
  • 10-year range: charges are escalated by randomly re-sampling the last 10 years of South East Queensland price changes (−7% to +22% a year); the range shows the middle 80% of 2,000 runs. It is not a prediction.
  • Batteries: specs come from manufacturer documents where available; each spec is labelled with its source. Check the exact model is on the Clean Energy Council approved list before relying on the rebate.
  • Estimates only, not advice. Check eligibility and the retailer's fact sheet before switching.
  • Data: plan data from the Australian Energy Regulator's Energy Product Reference Data (Consumer Data Right); weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0, based on Copernicus ERA5); postcode locations from Matthew Proctor's Australian Postcodes (CC BY 4.0); price history from AER Default Market Offer determinations and the ABS CPI.
